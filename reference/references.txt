   - Explains the rigid body dynamics of satellites and the design of PID controllers for attitude control.

2. Song, Y. et al. "Pulse-width pulse-frequency modulator for satellite attitude control." IEEE Transactions on Aerospace and Electronic Systems.
   - Provides standard formulations and tuning techniques for the PWPFM (Pulse-Width Pulse-Frequency Modulator), consisting of a first-order lag filter and a Schmitt trigger.

---------------------------------------------------------
TUNING DETAILS AND CALCULATIONS
---------------------------------------------------------

1. PID Controller Tuning (PD actually)
For a rigid spacecraft, the attitude dynamics model is a double integrator: Inertia (I) * angular_acceleration = Torque.
If we use a Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller, we can match the closed-loop system to the standard second-order system equation.

Given constraints:
*   Bandwidth: 0.05 Hz
*   Damping ratio: 0.9
*   Inertia: 10 kg*m^2

First, calculate the natural frequency (omega_n) in radians per second:
*   omega_n = 2 * pi * 0.05 = 0.314 rad/s

Then, calculate the gains:
*   Proportional Gain (Kp) = Inertia * (omega_n^2) = 10 * (0.314^2) = 0.98 Nm/rad
*   Derivative Gain (Kd) = Inertia * 2 * damping * omega_n = 10 * 2 * 0.9 * 0.314 = 5.65 Nm*s/rad

*(Note: Integral gain (Ki) is omitted because integrating attitude error in space usually causes massive overshoot known as "integral wind-up" due to the lack of gravity or atmospheric drag.)*

2. PWPFM Tuning
The PWPFM converts the continuous torque command from the PID into discrete on/off thruster pulses. It introduces a deadband (steady-state error) and a minimum pulse width (the shortest time a thruster is allowed to fire).

A. Designing the Deadband (Km and U_on)
The PWPFM will not fire unless the commanded torque generates a filter state that exceeds U_on. The equation for this deadband is:
Torque Deadband = U_on / Km

Target: Satellite stops within ~1 degree of the target.
*   1 degree is about 0.017 radians.
*   Commanded torque for 1 degree of error = Kp * 0.017 = 0.98 * 0.017 = 0.016 Nm.

So, U_on / Km needs to be roughly 0.016 Nm.
Chosen values: Km = 5.0 and U_on = 0.1, giving a deadband of 0.02 Nm (roughly 1.1 degrees of steady-state error).

B. Designing the Minimum Pulse Width (Tm and U_off)
Real RCS thrusters have a minimum firing time of about 20 to 50 milliseconds. The minimum pulse width (tau_min) of a PWPFM can be approximated by:
tau_min = Tm * ln( (Km * U_m + U_on) / (Km * U_m + U_off) )

*   We know U_m = 1.0 Nm (max thruster torque).
*   Chosen U_off = 0.03 (roughly one-third of U_on to provide good hysteresis and prevent rapid chatter).

Targeting a minimum pulse width of roughly 50 ms (0.05 seconds):
*   0.05 = Tm * ln( (5.0 * 1.0 + 0.1) / (5.0 * 1.0 + 0.03) )
*   0.05 = Tm * ln( 5.1 / 5.03 )
*   0.05 = Tm * 0.0138
*   Tm = 3.5 seconds

Summary of Final Parameters:
- Kp: 0.98
- Kd: 5.65
- Km: 5.0
- Tm: 3.5 s
- U_on: 0.1
- U_off: 0.03
