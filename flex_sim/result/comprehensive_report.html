Satellite Attitude Control

Comprehensive Simulation & Stability Analysis Report

1. Time-Domain Simulation Performance

The attitude control system was evaluated for a 50-degree step maneuver using a PWPFM (Pulse-Width Pulse-Frequency Modulator) to drive on-off RCS thrusters. We compared the baseline rigid satellite against a realistic satellite with flexible solar panel modes (0.5 Hz and 2.0 Hz) with and without compensation.

Rigid Satellite Baseline

The pure PD controller and PWPFM seamlessly drove the rigid satellite to the target angle, stabilizing exactly within the designed 1.1° deadband with 29 thruster pulses.

Flexible Satellite (Uncompensated)

Without Notch Filters, the "bang-bang" nature of the PWPFM thrusters continually excites the flexible solar panels, causing severe vibration and a destructive limit cycle where the thrusters chatter endlessly.

Flexible Satellite (Compensated, Step Command)

By adding the Notch Filter compensators, the modal displacements (eta) are quickly damped out. The maneuver is safely achieved in 35 pulses without any resonant limit cycles.

Extreme Maneuver: 2 Hz Square Wave Tracking

To further test the robustness of the system, we commanded a highly aggressive ±50° square wave at 2.0 Hz. This exactly matches the frequency of the second flexible mode, creating a worst-case resonance scenario.

Uncompensated Response

Without the Notch Filters, the system is subjected to severe high-frequency control actions. The thrusters chatter wildly, and the attitude response is highly degraded by the unmitigated structural vibrations.

Compensated Response (Notch Filters Active)

With the Notch Filters engaged, the controller intelligently "ignores" the 2.0 Hz command frequency component. The thrusters fire cleanly, completely suppressing the resonance while still attempting to track the aggressive maneuver safely.

Extreme Maneuver: 0.5 Hz Square Wave Tracking

We repeated the aggressive ±50° square wave test, this time at exactly 0.5 Hz. This perfectly matches the frequency of the first flexible mode, creating another severe resonant coupling scenario.

Uncompensated Response

Just like the 2.0 Hz case, the uncompensated system rapidly destabilizes. The energy from the aggressive maneuver couples directly into the solar panels, leading to intense vibrations and thruster chatter.

Compensated Response (Notch Filters Active)

The Notch Filters successfully remove the resonant 0.5 Hz energy from the torque command. The system smoothly tracks the aggressive 0.5 Hz command using clean thruster pulses, with virtually zero resonant vibration.

Low Frequency Maneuver: 0.04 Hz Square Wave Tracking

We finally tested a low-frequency ±50° square wave at 0.04 Hz. This frequency is just below the controller's design bandwidth (0.05 Hz) and well below the flexible mode frequencies, allowing the system enough time to settle during each half-cycle.

Uncompensated Response

Even at this low command frequency, the sharp edges of the square wave command act like a step input, broadband-exciting the structure. The uncompensated system still chatters at the resonant frequencies as it attempts to hold the target attitude.

Compensated Response (Notch Filters Active)

With the Notch Filters active, the system easily tracks the slow square wave. The sharp edges of the command are smoothed out at the problematic frequencies, preventing resonant excitation and leading to a clean, stable limit cycle around the target attitude.

Ultra-Low Frequency Maneuver: 0.005 Hz Square Wave Tracking

To conclude the tracking analysis, we tested an ultra-low frequency ±50° square wave at 0.005 Hz. With a period of 200 seconds, this is essentially a sequence of very widely spaced step commands, allowing us to observe the true steady-state limit cycle behavior between transitions.

Uncompensated Response

Because the sudden edges of the square wave command still contain high-frequency energy, the uncompensated system experiences massive structural excitation at every transition. It takes a long time for these vibrations to settle naturally, causing the thrusters to work erratically to maintain attitude.

Compensated Response (Notch Filters Active)

With the Notch Filters, the system performs a clean step maneuver at each command edge, rapidly settling into the designed minimum-pulse limit cycle. The solar panel vibrations are immediately damped out, demonstrating perfect steady-state performance.

Delay Stress Test: 0.005 Hz Square Wave Tracking with 100 ms Computation Delay

We ran the ultra-low frequency 0.005 Hz square wave tracking test again, but this time we intentionally increased the computation delay from 16 ms to 100 ms. Delay severely degrades phase margin, making this a critical stress test for our Notch Filter compensated system.

Uncompensated Response

The additional delay completely breaks the uncompensated system. The limit cycle amplitude grows, the thruster chattering becomes even more continuous and severe, and the solar panels undergo massive sustained vibrations, potentially leading to structural failure.

Compensated Response (Notch Filters Active)

Even with 100 ms of pure computational delay eating into the phase margin, the Notch Filters successfully stabilize the system! It handles the large step maneuvers and settles into a stable, tightly bounded limit cycle, proving the exceptional robustness of the compensator design.
